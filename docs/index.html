            Four steps through the payment gate

            A sluice lets water through only when the gate opens. Sluice lets a payment count only when Solana shows it, and sends the rest back.

            1. The merchant signs the invoice

              The merchant's Solana wallet signs amount, address and how it settles. Change one character and the checkout refuses the link.

            2. The customer pays with what they hold

              USDC on Base, ETH on Arbitrum, USDT on Tron, BTC and more. Connect a wallet, or scan a QR from any wallet or exchange.

            3. Solana has the last word

              The page reads the merchant's balance on Solana itself. A provider's "success" is only a hint.

            4. Short, late or failed payments go back

              They return to the payer on the chain they paid from. On 4 Nov, 0.20 USDC sent for a 0.25 invoice came back as 0.1976 USDC, and 0.30 sent for 0.25 returned the 0.037512 excess.

              Invoices that earn from the first second

              Payment rails leave money idle until someone moves it. With Sluice a customer on Base can pay an invoice and, in one atomic Solana transaction run through Aurora Intents Connect, the USDC is supplied to Kamino and the yield-bearing cUSDC lands in the merchant's own token account. The payer's account never holds the position, and the order's reference travels with the deposit, so the payment is findable from Solana alone.

              Sluice's fee rides in the same transaction. On these invoices the payer adds 0.5% on top, sent to Sluice's own account in the very transaction that settles into Kamino, so the merchant still gets exactly the invoice. The fee account and the rate are pinned in the checkout, which shows the fee before the wallet opens and refuses a transaction that asks for anything else.

              1. BasePayer signs once, sends USDC
              2. NEAR IntentsRoutes it to Solana
              3. KaminoSupplies USDC, mints cUSDC
              4. MerchantOwns the position

              Why the 4 Nov run took two transactions

              Its payer authorised Intents Connect with a Solana wallet, which signs ed25519, while the USDC sat on Base. Asked for a Connect execution funded from Base with a Solana signer, Aurora's API refused it (400, "refundTo is not valid"). So the USDC moved first, with a plain Aurora swap into the payer's own Connect account on Solana. Then the Solana wallet signed one Connect transaction that supplied Kamino, paid both fees and attached the reference.

              An EVM wallet holding the USDC on Base does both with one signature, as in the 1 Nov run. The checkout connects EVM wallets; Solana wallets pay by QR.

              For APIs and agents

              The same rail works without a browser. A Sluice API answers 402 Payment Required with its price in USDC on Solana. The buyer pays from any supported chain and calls again. The server delivers exactly once, only after it has read the USDC credit on Solana, and returns a signed receipt.

              A hosted one is live. Ask it for its price:

              curl -i https://sluice-api.swiftbotlab.workers.dev/api/report/9cYFzrmHRcsYzLhAWsNemzCy75ZhETn2px4MS8oDDReR

              Then create an order with your own refund address, send the exact amount it quotes from your own wallet, and call again with X-Sluice-Order. The receipt key is published at /.well-known/sluice.json. Every step, with the headless buyer that checks the quote against NEAR Intents' public record before it pays:

              What a merchant can check on a private payment

              Confidential Intents hides which wallet paid. For the 29 Oct run, Aurora's status says success and lists no hashes and no amounts. Three things stay checkable:

              • The credit, on Solana. 0.50 USDC reached the merchant's own USDC account in 44GN5e…tuB6Cj, read by Sluice from Solana, not from Aurora.
              • The payment, on Arbitrum. 0.000222930139609761 ETH went from the payer's wallet to the order's deposit address in 0x51fd…94564a, exactly the amount the quote asked for.
              • The quote, made before the deposit. 0.000222930139609761 ETH, which Aurora valued at $0.5201, for exactly 0.50 USDC, with 0.010005 USDC of Solana delivery and 45 bps of app fees listed. It is saved in the repository's evidence.

              What stays hidden is how the difference splits between the solver, Aurora and the fees. For the merchant that is acceptable: the hidden part changes only what the payer spends, which the payer saw in the quote before sending, never what the merchant receives, which Solana shows. If less than the invoice arrives, nothing counts as paid. Before a payer picks a private payment, the checkout says its quotes cannot be cross-checked in public.
